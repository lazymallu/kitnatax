Got an email about section 143(1)? Here is what it means

Short answer: it is not a penalty or a scrutiny notice. It is the department's automatic check of the tax return you filed, and almost everyone who files gets one.

It ends one of three ways: nothing to pay, a refund coming to you, or tax still to pay. Only the last needs action, usually within 30 days.

How to open it

The email comes from the Centralised Processing Centre (CPC) in Bengaluru with a locked PDF. The password is your PAN in small letters followed by your date of birth as DDMMYYYY, with no spaces.

Example: PAN ABCDE1234F, born 5 March 1990, gives abcde1234f05031990.

Lost the email? Log in to the portal and open e-File, Income Tax Returns, View Filed Returns, then pick the year. (official manual)

What to look at

The PDF has a table with two columns: what you said in your return, and what the department worked out. Ignore everything except the last line.

Example intimation, last linesSample figures
Tax payable on total income₹98,750
Taxes paid (TDS and others)₹1,10,000
Refund due₹11,250

If both columns match, the department agrees with you. If they differ, the line that differs tells you why.

What to do, by result

Pick what your intimation shows:

You are done

Your return has been accepted as filed. Save the PDF with your Form 16 for at least 6 years. No reply is needed.

Your refund is on its way

  1. Check the refund goes to a pre-validated bank account (one the portal has checked against your PAN). You can see and add accounts in your profile on the portal.
  2. Track it at e-File, Income Tax Returns, View Filed Returns, View Details. (official manual)
  3. If the status says the refund failed (often a closed account or a name mismatch), go to Services, Refund Reissue, Create Refund Reissue Request, choose a validated account and e-verify. (official manual)
  4. Refund late or missing? See refund not received for every status and its fix.
  5. If the refund is smaller than you expected, compare the two columns. A smaller refund usually means some TDS was not matched. See "If you think it is wrong" below.

Respond before the deadline

  1. Find the line where the two columns differ. Common causes: TDS your employer deducted but did not report, interest income you left out, or a deduction claimed without proof.
  2. Log in and open Pending Actions, Response to Outstanding Demand, then Submit Response. (official manual)
  3. Choose one:
    Demand is correct: pay it through Pay Now. Once you say it is correct, you cannot dispute it later.
    Disagree, fully or partly: pick the reasons and add details. Pay any part you agree with.
  4. If you disagree because the department missed something in your return, also file a rectification (below). If the mistake was yours, a revised return is the fix, if the deadline for one has not passed.
  5. Do not ignore it. An unpaid demand can be taken out of a future refund.

Your deadlines

30 days is the usual window to respond to a demand. Rectification with CPC is open for 4 years from the end of the financial year in which the intimation was issued. (official FAQ)

If you think it is wrong

Rectification is a request to correct a mistake the department made while processing, such as missing TDS that appears in your Form 26AS. It is not for changing your income or adding a new claim.

  1. Log in and open Services, Rectification, New Request. Pick the assessment year. (official manual)
  2. Choose the type: Reprocess the return to have it checked again, Tax credit mismatch correction if TDS was missed, or Return data correction to fix figures.
  3. Submit and e-verify. You get a 15-digit reference number. Track it under Rectification Status.
  4. Only one request per year can be open at a time, and it cannot be edited once sent.

When to get help

Get a chartered accountant or a filing service if the demand is large for you, if you do not understand which line differs, or if a second notice arrives with a different section number such as 142(1) or 148. Those are not automatic checks.
