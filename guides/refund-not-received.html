Income tax refund not received? Find where it is stuck

Short answer: log in to the e-filing portal and read the status of your return. It tells you which of five things has happened, and each has its own fix.

Most refunds that never arrive have a bank account problem or a return that was never e-verified. Both take a few minutes to fix online.

Step 1: read your status

Log in and open e-File, Income Tax Returns, View Filed Returns, pick the year, then View Details. (official manual)

Can't log in? On the portal home page, Income Tax Return (ITR) Status shows the status with just your acknowledgement number and a mobile OTP. (official manual)

Step 2: pick what it says

Your return is not finished until you e-verify it

  1. Nothing is processed and no refund is paid until the return is e-verified. You have 30 days from filing.
  2. Log in, open e-File, Income Tax Returns, e-Verify Return and use an Aadhaar OTP or net banking. (official manual)
  3. If you are past 30 days, e-verify anyway; the portal asks for a reason for the delay. The day you verify counts as the day you filed, so late filing rules can apply. A return never verified is treated as invalid. (official FAQ)

The department still has time

  1. Processing is automatic and often quicker, but the law allows up to 9 months after the end of the financial year you filed in. Use the date box below to see your last date. (official table)
  2. Meanwhile, make sure a bank account is pre-validated. Open My Profile, My Bank Account. Only a validated account can receive a refund, and validation takes 10 to 12 working days. (official FAQ)
  3. Check your PAN is linked to Aadhaar. A refund to an inoperative PAN fails. (official manual)
  4. Watch your email for a notice asking for something, such as a defective return notice. A notice you don't answer can hold up the refund.

Check the account it went to

  1. View Details shows that the refund was issued. Allow a few working days for the bank to credit it.
  2. Check which account was nominated in My Profile, My Bank Account. It may be an old account you no longer watch.
  3. If it still has not arrived, raise a grievance (below) with the date and amount from View Details. Do not file a new return.

Fix the account, then ask for it again

  1. Usual causes: a closed account, the name on the account not matching your PAN, a wrong IFSC, an account not pre-validated, or a PAN not linked to Aadhaar. (official manual)
  2. Add or re-validate a savings or current account under My Profile, My Bank Account and wait until it shows as validated. (official manual)
  3. Open Services, Refund Reissue, Create Refund Reissue Request, choose the validated account and e-verify. Save the transaction ID. (official manual)

An old demand took some or all of it

  1. If you owe tax for an earlier year, the department can set your refund against it. The status shows partially adjusted or full refund adjusted. (official manual)
  2. Open Pending Actions, Response to Outstanding Demand to see which year and how much. (official manual)
  3. If you disagree, submit a response there. A demand you do not answer is confirmed and taken from your refund. (official FAQ)
  4. If the refund is smaller because some TDS was not counted, see our guide to the 143(1) email for rectification.

How long the department has

If you e-verified more than 30 days after filing, enter the date you e-verified instead. The 9-month rule comes from the department's table of time limits. (official table)

Interest on a late refund

You do not have to ask for it. For returns under the 1961 Act, the department adds simple interest of about 0.5% a month, usually counted from 1 April after the year ends if you filed on time. No interest is paid if the refund is less than 10% of your tax. The interest is taxable in the year you get it.

Still stuck? Raise a grievance

  1. Log in and open Grievances, Submit Grievance. (official manual)
  2. Pick the department CPC-ITR and the category Refund. Choose the Income-tax Act, 1961 for income earned before April 2026, even if you filed later.
  3. Write at least 100 characters: the year, the amount, what the status says and what you have already tried. Note the acknowledgement number.

When to get help

Get a chartered accountant or a filing service if the refund was adjusted against a demand you do not recognise, if a grievance gets no useful answer in about a month, or if a notice arrives under 142(1), 143(2) or 148. Those are not automatic checks.
